# Code of Conduct — Tiny Crow

Tiny Crow is a small, open project, and it stays healthy only if the people
around it treat each other well. This document sets out, in plain language,
how we expect everyone to behave in this project's spaces, and what happens
when someone does not.

It is a community agreement, not a legal contract. It is also not code:
nothing in Tiny Crow reads or enforces this file automatically. It is
enforced by people — the maintainers — using the process in section 4
and section 5 below.

## 1. Who this covers

This code applies to everyone who takes part in Tiny Crow, including:

- Maintainers and reviewers
- Contributors, whether the contribution is code, docs, tests, or an
  issue report
- Anyone commenting, discussing, or asking for help in the repository's
  issues, pull requests, and discussions

It also applies when someone is clearly speaking or acting for Tiny Crow
in public — for example, from an official project account, or as an
appointed representative at an event.

It does not police anyone's private life. It covers behaviour in and
around this project, and behaviour that is aimed at this project's
community.

## 2. How we treat each other

### We do

- Assume good intent first, and ask a clarifying question before
  assuming the worst.
- Be kind and direct in reviews. Say what is wrong, why it matters,
  and what would fix it.
- Accept feedback gracefully, including when it disagrees with us, and
  say plainly when we made a mistake.
- Respect other people's time: search before opening a duplicate
  issue, give a minimal reproduction when reporting a bug, and keep
  discussions on topic.
- Respect privacy. Never share someone else's personal details,
  credentials, API keys, or private messages without their clear
  permission.
- Keep contributions honest and safe: do not submit code, prompts, or
  instructions designed to steal data, bypass permission checks, or
  harm someone else's machine or account.

### We do not

- Harass, threaten, insult, or demean anyone — in public or in private
  messages about this project.
- Use sexualised language or imagery, or make unwanted sexual
  advances of any kind.
- Troll, bait, or derail discussions, or turn a technical disagreement
  into a personal attack.
- Publish someone's private information, such as their address, phone
  number, email, or credentials.
- Spam the repository with duplicate, off-topic, or automated issues
  and pull requests.
- Claim someone else's work as our own, or misrepresent what a
  contribution does.

The short version: be decent, be specific, and leave the project a
little better than you found it.

## 3. What maintainers promise

Maintainers are held to the same standards as everyone else, and to
one more: they must enforce this code fairly.

That means maintainers will:

- Apply the same rules to everyone, including long-time contributors
  and themselves.
- Explain a moderation decision when asked, in as much detail as
  privacy allows.
- Keep a reporter's identity and private details confidential, and
  never share them with the person reported against.
- Act promptly, but not rashly: a serious report is looked into
  before a permanent decision is made, unless someone is in immediate
  danger of harm, in which case maintainers may act first and explain
  afterwards.

Maintainers may remove, edit, or reject a comment, issue, pull
request, or contribution that breaks this code. That is a moderation
decision about this project, not a judgement about a person's worth.

## 4. How to report a problem

If you see behaviour that breaks this code, report it through the
repository's issue tracker.

A useful report includes:

- What happened, in your own words
- Where it happened (a link to the issue, pull request, or comment)
- When it happened, if you know
- Who was affected, if you are comfortable saying so

Do not post private information in a public report. If your report
itself contains something sensitive — for example, a leaked credential
or a private message — describe what happened without quoting the
sensitive part, and say that details are available privately. The
maintainers will follow up through GitHub.

Every report is read, taken seriously, and reviewed fairly. You will
not be punished for reporting in good faith, even if the maintainers
ultimately decide that no rule was broken.

## 5. What happens when the code is broken

Maintainers choose a response that fits the seriousness and the
pattern of the behaviour. In general, the steps are:

| Step | When it is used | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Correction | A first, minor slip — for example, careless wording or an off-topic outburst | A private message explaining what was wrong and what to do instead. A public apology may be asked for, but it is not demanded as a punishment. |
| 2. Warning | A clear violation, or a repeat of behaviour already corrected | A formal warning, with a clear statement of what happens if it continues. Contact with the people involved may be paused for a set period. |
| 3. Temporary restriction | Serious or sustained behaviour that is harming the community | A temporary ban from participating in the project's spaces — issues, pull requests, discussions, and events — for a set period. |
| 4. Permanent ban | A pattern of serious violations, sustained harassment, or behaviour that makes the community unsafe | A permanent ban from all of the project's public spaces. |

Two exceptions to the ladder:

- If behaviour causes immediate harm — for example, publishing
  someone's private data or credentials — maintainers may skip
  straight to a temporary or permanent restriction.
- A decision can be appealed. If you believe a decision was wrong or
  unfair, say so through the issue tracker, explain why, and a
  maintainer who was not involved in the original decision will
  review it where possible.

## 6. Changes to this document

This code belongs to the project, so maintainers may update it as the
project grows. Changes are made in the open, in this file, like any
other change to the repository. The version in this file is the one
that applies — an older copy does not override it.

## 7. The one-line version

If you remember nothing else, remember this: treat people in this
project the way you would want to be treated when you are new,
tired, and trying your best.
