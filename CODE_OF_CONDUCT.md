# Tiny Crow Code of Conduct

Tiny Crow is a small, open project that depends on people treating one another well. This document explains expected behaviour in the project's spaces and how violations are handled.

This is a community agreement, not a legal contract. It is not code, and Tiny Crow does not read or enforce it automatically. Maintainers enforce it using the processes in sections 4 and 5 below.

## 1. Scope

This code applies to everyone taking part in Tiny Crow, including maintainers and reviewers; contributors of code, documentation, tests, or issue reports; and anyone commenting, discussing, or asking for help in the repository's issues, pull requests, or discussions.

It also applies when someone is clearly speaking or acting for Tiny Crow in public—for example, through an official project account or as an appointed representative at an event.

It does not police anyone's private life. It covers behaviour in and around this project and behaviour aimed at this project's community.

## 2. How we treat one another

### Expected behaviour

- Assume good intent first and ask a clarifying question before assuming the worst.
- Be kind and direct in reviews. Explain what is wrong, why it matters, and what would fix it.
- Accept feedback gracefully, including disagreement, and acknowledge mistakes plainly.
- Respect other people's time: search before opening a duplicate issue, provide a minimal reproduction when reporting a bug, and keep discussions on topic.
- Respect privacy. Do not share another person's personal details, credentials, API keys, or private messages without their clear permission.
- Keep contributions honest and safe. Do not submit code, prompts, or instructions designed to steal data, bypass permission checks, or harm someone else's machine or account.

### Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassing, threatening, insulting, or demeaning anyone, publicly or in private messages about this project.
- Using sexualised language or imagery, or making unwanted sexual advances of any kind.
- Trolling, baiting, or derailing discussions, or turning a technical disagreement into a personal attack.
- Publishing someone's private information, such as their address, phone number, email, or credentials.
- Spamming the repository with duplicate, off-topic, or automated issues and pull requests.
- Claiming someone else's work as your own or misrepresenting what a contribution does.

In short: be decent, be specific, and leave the project a little better than you found it.

## 3. Maintainer commitments

Maintainers are held to the same standards as everyone else and must also enforce this code fairly. They will:

- Apply the same rules to everyone, including long-time contributors and themselves.
- Explain moderation decisions when asked, as fully as privacy allows.
- Keep a reporter's identity and private details confidential and never share them with the person reported against.
- Act promptly, but not rashly: investigate a serious report before making a permanent decision, unless someone is in immediate danger of harm. In that case, maintainers may act first and explain afterwards.

Maintainers may remove, edit, or reject a comment, issue, pull request, or contribution that violates this code. This is a moderation decision about the project, not a judgement about a person's worth.

## 4. Reporting a problem

Report behaviour that violates this code through the repository's issue tracker.

A useful report includes what happened, in your own words; where it happened (a link to the issue, pull request, or comment); when it happened, if known; and who was affected, if you are comfortable saying so.

Do not include private information in a public report. If the report involves sensitive material—for example, a leaked credential or private message—describe what happened without quoting the sensitive part and say that details are available privately. Maintainers will follow up through GitHub.

Every report is read, taken seriously, and reviewed fairly. You will not be punished for reporting in good faith, even if maintainers ultimately decide that no rule was broken.

## 5. Responses to violations

Maintainers choose a response that fits the seriousness and pattern of the behaviour. The general steps are:

| Step | When used | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Correction | A first, minor slip, such as careless wording or an off-topic outburst | A private message explaining what was wrong and what to do instead. A public apology may be requested, but is not demanded as punishment. |
| 2. Warning | A clear violation or a repeat of behaviour already corrected | A formal warning explaining what happens if the behaviour continues. Contact with the people involved may be paused for a set period. |
| 3. Temporary restriction | Serious or sustained behaviour harming the community | A temporary ban from the project's spaces—issues, pull requests, discussions, and events—for a set period. |
| 4. Permanent ban | A pattern of serious violations, sustained harassment, or behaviour that makes the community unsafe | A permanent ban from all of the project's public spaces. |

There are two exceptions to this progression:

- If behaviour causes immediate harm—for example, by publishing someone's private data or credentials—maintainers may skip directly to a temporary or permanent restriction.
- A decision may be appealed. If you believe a decision was wrong or unfair, explain why through the issue tracker. Where possible, a maintainer not involved in the original decision will review it.

## 6. Changes to this document

This code belongs to the project, and maintainers may update it as the project grows. Changes are made openly in this file, like any other repository change. The version in this file is the one that applies; an older copy does not override it.

## 7. One-line summary

Treat people in this project the way you would want to be treated when you are new, tired, and trying your best.
